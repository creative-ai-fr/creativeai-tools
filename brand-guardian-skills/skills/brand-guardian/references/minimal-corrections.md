# Minimal corrections that preserve creative intent

Fix the documented mismatch at its smallest useful point. A correction is not a new creative brief.

## Method

1. State the asset's apparent central idea and objective in one sentence, or say when they are unclear.
2. Identify the exact noncompliant token, element, placement, or measurement.
3. Change only that element enough to meet the cited rule.
4. Say what remains intact: proposition, voice, composition, subject, rhythm, color concept, or other relevant intent.
5. If a local fix cannot resolve the issue, offer at most two alternatives and identify the trade-off; ask for a choice only when the intent or policy decision is genuinely unclear.

If the proposed replacement contains wording whose approval is not established by the supplied profile, label it as a candidate edit that removes the known mismatch. Do not imply that the replacement or the full asset is approved; name the remaining approval question when it matters.

## Examples

- Prohibited word “cheap” in “A cheap solution for everyone.” → suggest “An affordable solution for everyone.” Preserve the promise of accessibility; do not rewrite the entire ad.
- Logo clear-space violation → increase its surrounding margin to the cited minimum; do not redesign the layout if moving or scaling the logo is sufficient.
- A gradient is allowed in social motion but prohibited in print → retain the social motion version; adapt only the print deliverable.
- A layout departs from observed patterns but breaks no explicit rule → suggest no correction. Preserve the novel direction and label the departure only if useful.

Never silently weaken a confirmed requirement. If the minimal correction is blocked by the creative concept, explain the conflict and provide constrained options rather than presenting a compliant rewrite as the only answer.
